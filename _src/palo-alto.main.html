About Palo Alto

Why owners rent
in Palo Alto.

Palo Alto took its name from El Palo Alto, a tall redwood by San Francisquito Creek, and grew up alongside Stanford University after Leland and Jane Stanford founded the school in the 1890s. The Hewlett-Packard garage on Addison Avenue earned the city its reputation as the birthplace of Silicon Valley, and Stanford Research Park is still home to companies like HP and VMware today.

Away from the tech headlines, most of the city is quiet and residential, with historic homes in the north and mid-century Eichlers and ranch houses in Midtown and South Palo Alto. Families are drawn by the Palo Alto Unified School District, dozens of neighborhood parks, and open space that stretches from Foothills Park to the Baylands.

Who Rents Here

Who rents in Palo Alto

Knowing your renters means faster leasing and longer tenancies.

Stanford Community

Stanford is the city’s largest employer, so faculty, postdocs, visiting scholars, and Stanford Health Care staff are a big part of the rental market. Many come for 1–2 years and look for move-in ready homes close to campus.

Tech Professionals

Employees at Stanford Research Park, HP, VMware, and startups along University and California Avenues often want walkable homes near Caltrain.

Families

Many families rent while they wait to buy, choosing homes inside the Gunn or Palo Alto High attendance areas.

Local Rent Rules

Palo Alto rules,
handled for you.

A plain-English summary of the rules that apply to Palo Alto rentals. Not legal advice. We keep up with every change from City Council.

01

One-Year Lease

Landlords must offer a lease of at least 1 year (PAMC 9.68.030). A shorter lease can only be offered if the tenant turns down the 1-year lease first.

02

Just Cause Eviction

Palo Alto expanded just cause eviction protections to more renters than state law covers. The city’s protections are permanent, while the state’s (AB 1482) are set to end in 2030.

03

Relocation Assistance

Required for no-fault evictions. Properties with fewer than 10 units: 1 month’s rent (or waive the last month) within 15 days of the notice. Properties with 10+ units: about $8,400 (studio) to $20,400 (3+ bed), plus about $3,600 extra if the household is low-income, 60+, has a disability, or includes a minor. Amounts update every July 1.

04

Security Deposit

City limit is 1.5x monthly rent for unfurnished units (since July 2023). State law (AB 12, since July 2024) is stricter for most landlords at 1 month’s rent.

05

Rental Registry

Properties with 3+ units must register every year ($40/unit). 2026–27 registration is open Oct 1, 2026 – Jan 19, 2027. Rent changes, new tenants, and notices must be reported within 10 days. Late registration can result in a citation.

06

Mandatory Mediation

Many landlord-tenant disputes require a response through the free Palo Alto Mediation Program (650-856-4062).

07

Other Resources

Palo Alto Tenant Guide (English, Spanish, Chinese), Project Sentinel housing counseling, and California Apartment Association forms.
